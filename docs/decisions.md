| 8 | Break-even formula | | **With relative signal FF/2 and noise sqrt(1/(p0·N) + σ²): zA ∝ (FF/2)/sqrt(1/(p0N) + σ²) and zB ∝ (g·FF/2)/sqrt(1/(p0·r·N) + σ²), where g and r depend on the cutoff and on FF (#14). B beats A iff σ²(g² − 1) > (1/(p0N))(1/r − g²). If g²r ≥ 1, B is never worse; otherwise break-even depth N* = (1/r − g²) / (p0 σ² (g² − 1)). The prediction is compared with the simulation on the standardised separation (z_mean_t21 − z_mean_normal)/z_sd_normal of each method; the predicted ratio is B's separation ÷ A's.** Full derivation to be written by Maya in docs/break_even.md. | Without extra noise (σ = 0) the gain is g·√r, independent of depth (Task 04: matched within 0.8%). Depth matters only through σ, which is consistent with no significant gain at 5M reads but a significant one at 8.8M (Budis [1]), and a platform-dependent result at ~2.1M (Minárik [2]). **g²r falls as FF rises** (g shrinks while r barely changes), so whether a cutoff can hurt depends on FF: Yang's 156 bp window has g²r ≈ 1.20 at FF 4% and ≈ 1.03 at 15%, so it is never worse across 1–15%; the cutoff below which g²r < 1 (a real break-even depth exists) moves up with FF, from between 140 and 145 bp at FF 4% to ≈ 145 bp at 10% (g²r 0.94) and ≈ 150 bp at 15% (0.95). Break-even depths are read from breakeven_check.csv and Fig. 4, not from pre-simulation estimates. | 2026-11-01 |
