## Decisions

| # | Decision | Options considered | Choice | Reason | Date |
|---|----------|--------------------|--------|--------|------|
| 1 | Fragment-length distributions source | Rabinowitz supplementary data / digitised figure / parametric fit | **Exact per-bp counts behind Rabinowitz Fig. 1 (family G1), taken from the authors' Hoobari code** (github.com/nshomron/hoobari, `src/freebayes_patch.py`, commit 74ed24a). Saved as `data/raw/rabinowitz_G1_length_counts.csv`. Processing: drop length 0; replace the 101 bp bin by the mean of its neighbours (read-length artifact); keep 36–500 bp; normalise. | Matches the proposal (Rabinowitz [3]); exact counts instead of a digitised figure. Validated: the counts reproduce G1's published FF (30.1% vs 30.2% in Rabinowitz Table 1). Realism check: a 40–156 bp window keeps ~20% of reads and raises FF ~2.2–2.5×, in line with Yang [7] (75% discarded, FF ~doubled). **Limitation:** one pregnancy, sampled at 38 weeks (NIPT is done at 10–20 weeks). | 2026-10-26 |
| 2 | Read length and fragment length range | 2×36, 2×50, 2×75, 2×100 | **2×36 bp paired-end; fragment lengths 36–500 bp** | 2×36 matches typical NIPT runs (Yu [8] 2×36; Yang [7] 2×37). Reads can't exceed the fragment, so 36 bp is the minimum length (<0.06% of fragments are lost). 500 bp matches Rabinowitz's own max length and Bowtie2's default maximum insert (-X 500); excludes ~0.8% maternal / 0.1% fetal fragments. | 2026-10-26 |
| 3 | Pool size and sampling scheme | fresh fragments per patient / disjoint sub-pools, sampling with replacement / count-level simulation | **Map one pool of 10M maternal + 10M fetal fragments and use it only to estimate mappability: survival probability by length, S(l), and chromosome share of survivors, q(c). Each virtual patient is an independent multinomial draw of N surviving fragments from P_o(chrom, length) ∝ P_o(length)·S(length)·q(chrom), mixed by FF.** Patients are stored as a 2 × 465 count matrix (chr21 / other autosomes × length 36–500 bp). | Disjoint finite sub-pools (proposal wording) give each set a slightly different chr21 share by chance, which shifts all test z-scores against the reference by ~√(N·(1/P_ref + 1/P_test)) SD: ≈0.8 SD at 10M depth with 30M-fragment pools. That is an artifact (real patients share one genome) and it grows with depth, so it would bias the break-even result. Independent draws from one model remove it, keep steps 1–3 (Rabinowitz lengths, GRCh38, Bowtie2, MAPQ ≥ 40) intact, honour the intent of "no fragments shared between sets", and are fast enough for thousands of patients per condition. Task 01: 100k pairs map in ~5 s, 86% survive. **To state in Methods as a deviation from the proposal.** | 2026-10-28 |
| 4 | Patients per condition | | **Reference 2,000 normals; training 10,000 normal + 2,000 T21; calibration 10,000 normals; test 10,000 normal + 2,000 T21**, per FF × depth × noise condition. Each group has its own seed key `(1, i_ff, i_depth, i_noise, i_group)`. | Cheap under #3 (2,000 patients at 10M depth in ~0.07 s). The calibration normals are used only to set the tree threshold (#5); 10,000 of them allow 13 false positives at the target FPR. 10,000 test normals estimate an FPR near 0.135% with ~13 expected false positives (95% CI roughly 0.07–0.22%); 2,000 T21 give sensitivity to about ±1%. 10,000 training normals give the trees a large, balanced-weight training set. | 2026-10-30 |
| 5 | FPR handling | report observed FPR + CI / fix specificity | **Target FPR = 0.135%, the one-sided tail of z > 3. A and B: z > 3. Trees: fitted on the full training set; candidate thresholds are the final tree's own leaf probabilities with the rule 'positive if p ≥ t'; t = the smallest candidate whose FPR on the calibration normals is ≤ the target; the achieved value is reported as `calib_fpr`. If no leaf meets the target, the tree flags nobody (`nobody_positive`). All methods are also compared at exactly the target FPR via `sens_at_fpr`: test sensitivity read from the ROC, linearly interpolated between the two nearest points. Wilson 95% CIs for all observed rates.** | Follows the proposal (tree cutoff matched to method A's FPR). Unseen calibration normals give an unbiased threshold. An out-of-fold threshold was tried first but did not carry over to the refitted tree, whose leaf values differ: in the milestone, C1 flagged nobody. Shallow trees have few distinct outputs, so `sens_at_fpr` keeps the comparison fair; an interpolated ROC point corresponds to choosing randomly between two thresholds (Fawcett 2006). | 2026-10-30 |
| 6 | Size-selection cutoff for Method B | literature value / chosen on training set | **Main: fixed 40–156 bp window (Yang [7]). Extra analysis (B-opt): upper cutoff chosen on the training set from 120–170 bp, lower bound 40.** | The fixed window answers the question as labs practise it and matches the realism check; B-opt checks whether a better cutoff would change the conclusions, and gives B the same tuning chance as the trees. | 2026-10-28 |
| 7 | Noise model: where σ is applied and its values | per-patient shift on chr21 share / separate noise per length / none | **Per patient, ε ~ N(0, σ) multiplies the whole chr21 row (all lengths) by (1 + ε), then renormalise and draw counts. Applied to every group. σ (relative SD of the chr21 share) = 0 (none), 0.15% (low), 0.35% (realistic).** | Matches the proposal ('a small random shift to each patient's chromosome 21 proportion'). Realistic value from Minárik [2]: chr21 CV after GC correction was 0.70–0.71% at ~2.1M reads; removing the counting part (0.62% at that depth) leaves ~0.33–0.35% of extra, depth-independent variation (0.56–0.70% before GC correction). 'Low' ≈ half, a well-optimised lab. Yang [7], cited for this in the proposal, does not report a CV, so Minárik is used instead. **Assumption/limitation:** the same relative σ applies to size-selected fragments; if size selection added its own bias (e.g. GC), B would do worse than modelled. | 2026-10-30 |
| 8 | Break-even formula | | **With relative signal FF/2 and noise sqrt(1/(p0·N) + σ²): zA ∝ (FF/2)/sqrt(1/(p0N) + σ²) and zB ∝ (g·FF/2)/sqrt(1/(p0·r·N) + σ²). B beats A iff σ²(g² − 1) > (1/(p0N))(1/r − g²). If g²r ≥ 1, B is never worse; otherwise break-even depth N* = (1/r − g²) / (p0 σ² (g² − 1)).** Full derivation to be written by Maya in docs/break_even.md. | Without extra noise (σ = 0) the gain is g·√r, independent of depth (milestone: predicted 1.09, observed 5.45/4.95 = 1.10). Depth matters only through σ, which explains why studies found no significant gain at 3.1M/5M reads but a significant one at 5.8M/8.8M (Minárik [2], Budis [1]). Yang's window has g²r ≈ 1.2 (never worse, gain grows with depth); cutoffs below ~145 bp have g²r < 1 and a real break-even depth (≈2M for 135 bp, ≈5M for 130 bp at realistic σ). | 2026-10-30 |
| 9 | Decision-tree features (C1, C2) | | **C1: zA. C2: zA + z_short + z_weighted.** z_short (Yu [8]): proportion of fragments ≤150 bp on chr21 minus that on the other autosomes, z-scored against the reference normals. z_weighted (Rabinowitz [3]): chr21 fraction with each fragment weighted by `w(l) = FF·p_f(l) / (FF·p_f(l) + (1−FF)·p_m(l))`, using the condition's true FF and the post-mappability length distributions (normal fetal matrix), z-scored against the reference normals. B's z-score deliberately excluded from C2. | C1 vs C2 isolates the value of length information; each length feature comes from a cited study. `w(l)` is P(fetal \| length): Rabinowitz assign each fragment the fetal fraction observed at its length and approximate it as total FF × a length ratio; our formula is the exact form of the same quantity. Excluding zB keeps C2 a 'keep all fragments' method. **Limitation:** using the true FF is idealised; a lab has to estimate it, so z_weighted may do somewhat worse in practice. | 2026-10-30 |
| 10 | One tree per condition vs one tree overall | | **One tree per FF × depth × noise condition. Max depth from {3, 4, 5} by stratified 5-fold CV on the training set, chosen by mean AUC (smallest depth if tied); the tree is then refitted on the full training set. Trees stay as proposed, with no tail-specific changes.** | Same per-condition setting as the z-score reference, so the comparison with A and B is like for like. Shallow trees give only a few distinct scores, so their cutoff cannot sit exactly at the target FPR and at low signal can flag nobody; this is a real property of the proposed method and is discussed in the paper rather than engineered away. | 2026-10-30 |
| 11 | Chromosomes included | all / autosomes only | **Autosomes chr1–chr22** | Fetal sex is not modelled; sex chromosomes and chrM would add sex-dependent noise to the reference. Standard practice for chr21 z-scores. | 2026-10-26 |
| 12 | Definition of depth and FF | before / after the mapping filter | **Both defined on fragments that pass the MAPQ ≥ 40 filter** | Matches what a lab actually counts (unique mapped reads) and measures (FF of the analysed reads). | 2026-10-28 |
| 13 | Detection limit | first crossing / stays above; point estimate / CI bound | **The FF at which sensitivity crosses 95% for the last time (stays ≥ 95% at every higher grid FF), linearly interpolated between grid FFs (1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 8, 10, 15%). Main: point estimate from `sens` (each method at its real cutoff) and a conservative estimate from `sens_lo`; reported as '< 1%' / '> 15%' outside the grid. Check: the same limit from `sens_at_fpr` (point estimate only), flagged if it is more than one grid step from the main limit; for this comparison '< 1%' counts as 1% and '> 15%' as 15%.** | 'Stays above' avoids a lucky crossing from sampling noise; interpolation separates methods that would otherwise share a grid FF. `sens` is what a lab running the method would get and follows the proposal; differences in information are captured threshold-free by AUC, and the check shows how much of any gap comes from the trees' coarse cutoffs. | 2026-10-30 |
| 14 | g and r (inputs to the break-even formula, #8) | sampled / exact | **Computed exactly from the post-mappability length distributions (normal fetal matrix), with no sampling:** g = FF within 40–156 bp ÷ overall FF; r = fraction of fragments in 40–156 bp. | Exact values remove sampling noise; they match the sampled patients (Task 02 QC: FF 4%: g = 2.43, r = 0.203; FF 10%: g = 2.23, r = 0.221). | 2026-10-30 |
| 15 | Extra analyses beyond the proposal | | **(a) Method B at fixed cutoffs 120, 130, 135, 140, 145, 150, 165 bp (main B stays 156); (b) W: z_weighted > 3 without a tree; (c) W_fixedFF: W with weights computed at FF = 10% for all conditions; (d) observed z-ratio zB/zA compared with the formula (#8).** | (a) maps where hard size selection stops paying off, which the formula predicts happens only for strict cutoffs; (b) separates the value of length weighting from the tree; (c) tests the 'true FF' limitation of #9 (at low FF the FF largely cancels out of the weight); (d) is the proposal's break-even check, done on z-scores because sensitivity saturates near 100%. All reuse the same patients, so they cost seconds. | 2026-10-30 |
