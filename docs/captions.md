Figure 3. Difference in sensitivity between size selection (B, 40–156 bp) and all fragments (A), in percentage points: sensitivity of B minus that of A, both at FPR 0.135% (read from the test ROC). Rows: fetal fraction; columns: depth (fragments passing MAPQ ≥ 40); one panel per noise level. Blue: B better; red: B worse. ## Figure 4

Output: `results/figures/fig4_breakeven` · script: `src/evaluate/figures/fig4_breakeven.py`

Figure 4. Observed and predicted gain of size selection at FF 4%: ratio of standardised separations sep(B at upper cutoff c)/sep(A), sep = (mean z of T21 − mean z of normals)/SD of normals, for c = 130, 140, 150 and 156 bp (B). Points: simulation; lines: break-even formula (decision #8) with the exact g and r of each cutoff at FF 4% and p0 = q(chr21). Dashed: B = A. Without extra noise the ratio is g·√r at every depth (Fig. 2e); noise lifts it towards g as depth grows, so strict cutoffs that lose at low depth win at high depth. Predicted break-even depths: 130 bp at low noise, 29M; 140 bp at low noise, 3.8M; 130 bp at realistic noise, 5.4M; 140 bp at realistic noise, 0.7M; none for 150 and 156 bp (g²r ≥ 1). Over all 768 FF × depth × noise × cutoff combinations (breakeven_check.csv), the median |relative error| is 0.9% (95th percentile 3.5%); without the 56 combinations where A's separation is below 1, 0.9% (2.9%). A paired bootstrap of the test patients gives a sampling SD of the observed ratio of 0.9–2.3% (breakeven_ratio_noise.csv), so the typical error is within sampling noise. The mean signed error is +0.2% (t = 1.6), with no trend across FF or depth.

## Figure 5

Output: `results/figures/fig5_methods` · script: `src/evaluate/figures/fig5_methods.py`

Figure 5. Sensitivity at FPR 0.135% (read from the test ROC) against fetal fraction for all fragments (A), size selection 40–156 bp (B), length-weighted z-score (W) and the decision trees on the count z-score (C1) and on the count, short-fragment and length-weighted z-scores (C2), at realistic noise (σ = 0.35%); one panel per depth. Sensitivity at a common FPR compares how well each method ranks patients. Hollow markers: the tree's own trained threshold flags nobody in that condition (all at FF ≤ 6%); its actual sensitivity there is 0%, which is what the detection limits in Table 1 use. C1 falls below A although it uses only A's z-score: a shallow tree has few distinct scores, so its ROC is coarse and the interpolated sensitivity is lower. W uses the true FF in its weights; W with the weights fixed at FF 10% in every condition gives almost identical results (sensitivity at FPR 0.135% within 5 points, mean difference -0.2; detection limits within 0.1 percentage points of FF), so this is not a real limitation. Dashed grey: 95% (detection-limit target); dotted grey: FF 4% (commercial minimum). The sampling SE of a difference in sensitivity at FPR 0.135% between two methods is 0.1–7.7 points (paired bootstrap of B − A, Fig. 3), largest on the steep part of the curves.
